# New Jersey — Medical Interpreter Requirements

Reference for building the NJ institutional standard (e.g. University Hospital,
Newark). Verified against primary sources; current as of 2026-06. This is a
working reference, not legal advice.

## Bottom line

**New Jersey does not license medical interpreters — neither spoken-language nor
sign-language (ASL).** There is no NJ "medical interpreter license" or state
registry that a healthcare interpreter must hold to practice. NJ regulates
interpreters only in specific **non-medical** settings (courts, K-12 schools).

For healthcare, the operative requirements are:
1. The **federal floor** — Section 1557 of the ACA, Title VI, and the ADA (a
   competency-based *qualified interpreter*, free of charge). University Hospital
   takes Medicare/Medicaid, so 1557 and Title VI apply.
2. **NJ hospital licensing** — N.J.A.C. 8:43G (Patient Rights), which puts the
   duty on the **hospital** to provide interpreter services, not on the
   interpreter to hold a credential.

National certification (CCHI / NBCMI for spoken; RID / NIC for ASL) is the de
facto quality benchmark, **not** an NJ legal requirement.

## Spoken-language medical interpreters

- **No NJ state license or certification to practice.** NJ does not require
  spoken-language medical interpreters to be state-licensed or state-certified.
- **NJ hospital rule — N.J.A.C. 8:43G-4.1 (Patient Rights):**
  - (a)(5): the patient has the right "to receive, as soon as possible, the
    services of a translator or interpreter to facilitate communication between
    the patient and the hospital's health care personnel."
  - (a)(27): the patient-rights summary must be provided "in the patient's
    native language if 10 percent or more of the population in the hospital's
    service area speak that language," and posted conspicuously.
  - The regulation does **not** require the interpreter to hold any state
    license/certification — only that the hospital provide the service.
  - Note: 8:43G's text does not itself say "free of charge"; the no-cost duty
    comes from federal 1557/Title VI (and is typically restated in the hospital's
    Patient Bill of Rights).
- **De facto benchmark (not state-mandated):** national certification — CCHI
  (CoreCHI / CHI-Spanish, NCCA-accredited) or NBCMI — plus a 40-hour healthcare
  interpreter course (e.g. Rutgers/NJHA).

## Sign-language (ASL) medical interpreters

- **No NJ statewide license to practice as an ASL interpreter.** NJ monitors
  interpreters through the Division of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DDHH),
  which maintains directories and refers interpreters; interpreters DDHH refers
  must hold **RID certification** — but that is a referral condition, not a
  general practice license.
- **Medical setting:** governed by the ADA effective-communication standard and
  1557's qualified-interpreter requirement, plus the same N.J.A.C. 8:43G-4.1
  hospital duty above. No NJ medical ASL license exists.
- **Where NJ *does* mandate an ASL credential (non-medical context):**
  - **Courts / legal proceedings:** sign-language interpreters must be RID-
    certified and listed with DDHH (NJ Courts Language Services; NJ sign-language
    interpreting statute, N.J.S.A. 34:1-69.x).
  - **K-12 education:** the NJ Dept. of Education "Educational Interpreter"
    endorsement is required to interpret for deaf/hard-of-hearing students;
    practitioners must meet an EIPA performance threshold (NJ minimum 3.5/5.0).
- **Pending legislation:** NJ has periodically introduced ASL-interpreter bills
  (e.g. S1771 — ASL at public meetings), but none establishing a *medical* ASL
  interpreter license has been enacted. (Flag: re-check currency before relying
  on this.)

## Implication for the AALB standard

- Do **not** require a (nonexistent) NJ state interpreter license in the
  University Hospital standard.
- Anchor the standard on: the 1557/ADA *qualified interpreter* definition; the
  N.J.A.C. 8:43G hospital duties (provide interpreters as soon as possible,
  no-cost per federal law, notices/vital-document translation at the 10%
  service-area threshold); and **national certification (CCHI/NBCMI for spoken,
  RID/NIC for ASL) as the assessed quality benchmark.**
- ASL court/education credential rules are out of scope for the hospital standard
  but worth noting if the client also operates in those settings.
